# Customer Psychology

1. Define the buyer's real situation.
2. Identify functional, emotional, and social jobs-to-be-done.
3. Separate stated objections from inferred underlying risks.
4. Use real customer language; label inferred motivations as hypotheses.
5. Map feature to practical benefit to emotional relevance.
6. Address trust, quality, delivery, returns, compatibility, and price transparently.
7. Avoid shame, fearmongering, fake urgency, and manipulative vulnerability targeting.

Output: buyer snapshot, buying triggers, objections, message hierarchy, proof required, and copy angles.
